What's Actually Happening Inside Aging Arizona, AZ HVAC Systems


A meaningful share of homes across Arizona, AZ was built between the 1960s and the 1990s, before modern energy codes reshaped how residential buildings are insulated, sealed, and mechanically ventilated. The ductwork installed in those homes was sized and designed for the equipment of that erlower-efficiency systems that moved air at different pressures and volumes than current high-efficiency equipment does.

That original ductwork is still in most of those homes today.

When a homeowner in Arizona, AZ upgrades to a new high-efficiency system but retains the original ductwork, the mismatch between system design airflow and duct capacity creates static pressure problems. The new equipment works against a distribution system that's creating resistance, which forces it to run harder, consume more electricity, and deliver less consistent airflow to rooms farthest from the air handler. Many Arizona, AZ homeowners replace equipment expecting a sharp drop in their utility bill, and the savings are smaller than expected. Ductwork is almost always the reason.

The Degradation Curve You Can't See


An HVAC system doesn't fail all at once. It degrades in ways that are invisible until they show up on a utility statement or a thermostat that won't settle.

Evaporator coils (the indoor heat-exchange surface) accumulate a thin film of particulate that progressively insulates the coil, reducing its ability to absorb heat from the passing air. The system runs longer to achieve the same cooling. Condenser coils on outdoor units develop the same problem from the outside. Refrigerant charge decreases slowly through microscopic leaks, a 10% undercharge can reduce system efficiency by 20% or more before anyone notices. Blower motor bearings wear and begin drawing more current. Capacitors weaken, causing motors to start under abnormal mechanical stress cycle after cycle.

None of these failures announce themselves with clarity. They just cost you money, every cycle, across every season in Arizona, AZ.

Leeway Heating & Air has evaluated enough Arizona, AZ HVAC systems to know where each of these degradation patterns tends to hide and how to quantify what they're actually costing a homeowner before recommending anything.

We provide itemized written quotes before any installation is authorized.My filter is clean and the thermostat seems fine. Do I really need a professional evaluation? A clean filter and a functioning thermostat rule out about 5% of what can go wrong with an HVAC system. The other 95% involves refrigerant charge, coil condition, electrical component health, duct integrity, blower performance, and (in gas heating equipment) heat exchanger condition. None of those are things a filter swap or thermostat reset can address.
