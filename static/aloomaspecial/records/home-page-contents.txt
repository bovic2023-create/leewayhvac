What Real Diagnostic Work Looks Like<h2>
The most common reason HVAC repairs get repeated is misdiagnosis. A technician replaces the most likely component without confirming it was actually the problem. Two weeks later, the system fails again (differently this time) because the underlying cause was never touched.
We measure refrigerant charge with calibrated manifold gauge sets rather than estimating by how the system "feels." We test capacitors and contactors with actual electrical meters rather than replacing them on suspicion. When airflow at the registers doesn't match the system's rated output, we measure duct static pressure and trace the restriction to its source. Thermal cameras go on ductwork and air handlers when the numbers at the outlet don't add up.
The diagnostic fee you pay covers the time required to do this correctly. The written repair quote that follows is based on what we actually found, not on what's common, not on what generates the largest ticket.

Installation Built Around What Your Specific Home Needs<h2>
A widespread installation mistake (and it happens constantly across the industry) is sizing a new system by replacing whatever was there before. The old system might have been oversized when it was installed, or undersized for a home that's been modified since, or correctly sized for conditions that no longer apply.
Leeway Heating & Air performs Manual J load calculations on every new installation. This is the ACCA-standard engineering method for determining actual heating and cooling load, it accounts for your home's square footage, ceiling height, insulation values, window area and orientation, infiltration rates, and local climate zone. It produces a precise BTU-per-hour number that tells us exactly what the new system needs to deliver.
An oversized system short-cycles, starting and stopping repeatedly without completing full conditioning runs. This is the primary driver of premature compressor failure, and it leaves homes cold but damp because the unit never runs long enough to remove humidity from the air. An undersized system runs continuously and never quite reaches setpoint on the worst days. A correctly sized system runs in longer, efficient cycles and maintains consistent temperature and humidity throughout its life.
We install and service equipment from manufacturers with documented efficiency ratings and genuine warranty support: Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Lennox, and Mitsubishi Electric for ductless systems. Our technicians are NATE-certified, they passed independent, third-party exams on real HVAC knowledge, not just in-house training and all refrigerant work is performed by EPA 608-certified technicians. Maintenance That Finds Problems Before They Become Failures<h2>
Most HVAC breakdowns are preventable. Evaporator and condenser coils accumulate a layer of dust and debris that acts as insulation, reducing heat transfer and forcing the system to work harder for the same output. Drain lines develop biofilm that eventually backs up, triggering float switches that shut the system down without warning. Capacitors (the components that help motors start and sustain operation) weaken gradually before failing suddenly, usually on the hottest or coldest day of the year.
Our maintenance visits cover coil cleaning on both indoor and outdoor surfaces, blower wheel inspection and cleaning, drain line flushing, electrical connection tightening, capacitor testing with calibrated meters, refrigerant pressure verification against manufacturer specification, and a full operational test in every mode. You receive a written record of every measurement taken. If something is trending toward failure, we flag it and then you decide whether to address it now or plan for it later.
Calling (833) 435-6630) before something breaks is almost always less disruptive and less expensive than calling after.

My system still uses R-22 refrigerant. What does that mean for my options? R-22 has been fully phased out of domestic production under EPA regulations, it is no longer manufactured in the U.S. What remains is recycled stockpile material, which is expensive and increasingly limited. For R-22 systems with refrigerant leaks, we provide a side-by-side cost comparison: the real cost to locate, repair, and recharge versus the cost of replacing with current-generation equipment that qualifies for federal tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act. For most R-22 systems older than 14 or 15 years, the math points toward replacement. For systems in otherwise good mechanical condition, repair can still make sense. We give you both numbers.
