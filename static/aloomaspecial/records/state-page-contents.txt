The Degradation Curve You Can't See<h2>
An HVAC system doesn't fail all at once. It degrades in ways that are invisible until they show up on a utility statement or a thermostat that won't settle.
Evaporator coils (the indoor heat-exchange surface) accumulate a thin film of particulate that progressively insulates the coil, reducing its ability to absorb heat from the passing air. The system runs longer to achieve the same cooling. Condenser coils on outdoor units develop the same problem from the outside. Refrigerant charge decreases slowly through microscopic leaks, a 10% undercharge can reduce system efficiency by 20% or more before anyone notices. Blower motor bearings wear and begin drawing more current. Capacitors weaken, causing motors to start under abnormal mechanical stress cycle after cycle.
None of these failures announce themselves with clarity. They just cost you money, every cycle, across every season in #State.

My filter is clean and the thermostat seems fine. Do I really need a professional evaluation? A clean filter and a functioning thermostat rule out about 5% of what can go wrong with an HVAC system. The other 95% involves refrigerant charge, coil condition, electrical component health, duct integrity, blower performance, and (in gas heating equipment) heat exchanger condition. None of those are things a filter swap or thermostat reset can address. If your #State utility bills are climbing, if the system is running longer per cycle than it used to, or if certain rooms consistently feel different from others, something else is happening. What does the SEER2 rating on new equipment actually mean for my #State utility bills? SEER2 (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio 2) measures cooling energy delivered per unit of electricity consumed, under 2023-updated testing conditions that more accurately reflect real operating conditions.
